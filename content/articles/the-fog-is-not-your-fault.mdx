You know the feeling. The day goes fine. The work gets done, the messages get answered, the food gets eaten. And underneath all of it there is a low hum you can never quite turn off, a sense that something is off and you cannot say what. It is not sadness. Sadness would at least be honest. This is quieter than that. You are technically fine. You are somehow insufficient. And you have started to suspect there is more going on beneath the surface than the routine will admit.

Here is the first thing I want you to know: it is not your fault.

What you are feeling has a name. We call it The Great Fog. It is old, older than your childhood, older than your parents' childhood, a kind of spell that convinced humanity it was small and separate and powerless. Naming it is the whole trick, because a nameless weight just feels like you failing at being a person. A named one can be fought. Beneath The Fog runs The Wyrd, the hidden web of fate that ties everything to everything else, and you have felt it. You felt it the time a piece of music broke something open in your chest and you could not explain why. You felt it the moment you met someone and knew, before a word, that they were kin.

We found ourselves standing in that same Fog, asking the same question, about 25 years ago. The answer we landed on was strange, and it turned out to be right: you fight synthetic slumber with living mythology. So we built one. Fire and swords and a flaming Viking ship for a stage. Fifteen years running a hundred-person camp in the Nevada desert, playing to crowds of ten thousand, from Vancouver Island down to California. We read the Toltec Agreements aloud under the stars to anyone who would sit still long enough. We built a temple to the Goddess and then we burned it. None of that is a metaphor. It happened, with our actual bodies, in the real world, in the heat and the dust. We are, yes, a bunch of fun-loving pagans who decided to take The Wyrd seriously. Twenty-five years later, this is what that decision looks like.

<PullQuote>We do not fight to destroy; we burn to illuminate.</PullQuote>

That same question, the one that got us into the desert, eventually turned into a game. A real one. We call it The Quest, and it is a mission, not a daydream. You take action in the world, and you bring back evidence that you did. The seven Arch-Parasites are your own fear, your own doubt, your own confusion. You beat them by making their opposite real, out here, where it counts. Yes, there are actual rune cards involved. If you want to go there, thequest.mythmaker.ca is the door.

This is not content. It is a record. It is called The Saga. Men who arrived at the fire angry and left something else. People who took a Quest card seriously and changed the direction of their life. The troupe has been on the road for two decades, and some of what happened out there deserves more than memory. We chose the word deliberately. Saga. Not blog, not journal. A story that is also a history, the shape of a people's life held together over time. And you should know, because it matters: you are not the first to arrive here. You have joined something already moving.

So if this stirred something, if The Fog felt familiar just now, if you have been half-sensing all along that there is more going on beneath the surface, then reach out your hand. We have been waiting for you. Have you seen the wolf?
